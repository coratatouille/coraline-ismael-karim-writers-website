The American Empire

Friday, September 25, 2026

In the collection of essays "Palestine: A Socialist Introduction", edited by Sumaya Awad and brian bean, Daphna Thier writes, "The [Israeli] working class has become dependent on the education, housing, and career opportunities that their participation in the [IOF] affords them" (93). I highly recommend reading this collection as its correlation between the working class struggle of Americans and many other societies under imperialist doctrines and Palestine's own liberation coincide greatly by the one consistent theme both entities share: oppression under an oppressor.

A few days ago my partner and I had dinner with two of our friends who frequent our social bike rides. Both are white-skinned individuals who work in corporate environments, though said corporate environments emphasize the conveniences that white folk will participate in even when claiming to be progressive or liberal. One works in health insurance; the other for the bomb manufacturer and war conspirator Northrup Grumman. Now, the participation within systems that contribute to the oppression of societies not within the American proxies does not only happen by white folk. It does, however, occur massively by those who take advantage of the systems that incentivize working there by providing higher wages and benefits albeit at the cost of life elsewhere. Distance from one's atrocities make it increasingly easy to participate in said atrocities. It's why many, like my one friend who works in health insurance, finds herself supporting Taylor Swift and Ukraine but finds no issue in giving grace for politicial candidates who consistently vote to send bombs to Israel or support a genocide. "Optics", as one may say.

Taylor Swift and Ukraine share two qualities that foment rage by the American corporate media which tell us how to feel when they are under attack: whiteness. And association. Ukraine isn't being carpet-bombed by America or its direct proxies right now. It's Russia. And Russia has always been a long-standing associate of the decades long Red Scare that America so badly prefers to use when America's own war crimes against its citizens are exposed that projection onto Russia seems almost commonplace and shared amongst every media regardless of its bicameral association. It also helps that Ukraine is white. And like most of America, the struggle of the white man is the shared struggle of us all.

Taylor Swift, on the other hand, is an effigy of neoliberalist whiteness, the vanity of goodness through hyper-feminization and a white woman's tears that scream unbridled innocence and a fine representation of capitalism through celebrity worship. Swift, though, is still somebody who maintains the authority of America. She does not comment on Israel's ongoing genocide of the Palestinian people, but years ago she found herself sobbing from being silent on Hillary Clinton in 2016, regretfully so, might I say. Though, like Ed Sheeran, what are the necessary optics to produce a reaction from your fans that isn't inherently proactive? Maintaining the status quo helps quite a bit. Like Ed Sheeran's recent neutrality and silence and complicity on the genocide of Palestinians, their profits and optics are far more important than genuine humanity. It's why Ed Sheeran did in fact participate in politics for Ukraine but not for Palestine while claiming to be apolitical. Safety and distance. Optics. And these celebrities who are mainly worshipped by the working class enable this lack of clairvoyance towards the American Empire by their visible associations. My friend who works in health insurance was furious on the ongoing war with Ukraine, but I found her mood changing into a calm passivity when I brought up the genocide of the Palestinians, and her commenting on where someone's morals are prioritized. She did not like me commenting on the fact that her being angry about Ukraine and irrationally calm about Palestine is likely due to the former's whiteness and the consistent racism of American society onto non-white oppressed indigenous people outside of America. Think of the silence of every brown and black person kidnapped by the terroristic ICE having little reaction from American society, yet Renee Good and Alex Prettis' deaths sparking vibrant resistance from the majority of our liberal society. Ah. I even know their names yet struggle to know the names of the brown and black folk killed and kidnapped and raped under ICE authority. Their information is invisible, forgotten, left to muddle beneath the cement of a thousand graves because a white person who is killed by American adjacency is criminal, but a black or brown one is an accidental tragedy that need not be worked up from.

So, is the fault of my white friends' associations with criminal entities that take away public tax dollars from the citizens and ruin their chances of universal healthcare or the six-figure career under a bomb manufacturer whose entire corporation is dependent on endless wars and arms dealings truly their own? When the only options that pay fair wages are those that are dependent on murder and killing and destruction, even the most moral of us are incentivized to give our souls. And I can't even say it's only white folk because indeed do middle-class black, brown, and asian folk participate in these criminal enterprises that depend on the labor of the working class and constant struggle of the impoverished all for their monetary benefit. So it is not entirely based on race but rather class is highly associated with the choices of our fellow neighbors.

When I read that passage from Thier's section, I immediately thought of America's military. I remembered the appearances from airmen and militants who visited our public school systems with their uniforms and pamphlets outside the cafeteria speaking to students I knew to be quite poor and impoverished yet under the impression that their circumstances weren't the fault of American inequality but for not giving themselves to the machine. The poorest of us would speak to these militants and airmen with such reverence that I was reminded of these memories because of the passage and further realized how private schools don't have these sort of happenings. Or at least I find it highly unlikely. I wouldn't know. I never went to one. But the military targeting institutions that have a class diaspora of both rich and poor students is a sad truth indeed. The rich kids at private schools don't need to be fed propaganda about the military; their parents will tell them whether or not they need to join the military, to join the military and then work under "defense" which is synonymous to machine-manufacturing that murders abroad and makes hundreds of thousands of dollars for an individual. The targeting of the poor students at public schools is more so to fill the infantry. To become pawns that are meant to die for the sanctity of the American empire at the promise of benefits, free schooling, free healthcare, free this and that that the common citizen whose same taxes pay for the military's expenses will never receive unless they, too, join the military. It is this artificial creation of comfort that is only inherent to those who submit themselves to the war machine, even more so to those who are the poorest and most vulnerable to believe it. And I empathize with them. I do. If trans people were still allowed to die for the war machine of America, I, too, would be in the airforce. Truly. I am being honest. Benefits and healthcare and education galore? Absolutely. And I know damn well my soul will be forfeit, and I'd be dead by now fighting for the genocides and wars of rich men who sit atop their thrones and consume their lavish delicacies from afar. In the comforts of their distance.

This country has forced a stream of labor to be at odds with one another so our jaws can cling into the necks of other working class members instead of the rich. We blame another for working for the military, or in health insurance, or for Northrup Grumman, when in reality it is those with the most wealth who have fabricated such a reality of labor which have removed us from making a living on more communal and cooperative ventures. So, Thier's passage on how the Israeli working class upholds racism and Zionism that belittles the wages of the Palestinians because in order for the Israeli working class to maintain their comforts under the Israeli Occupation Forces shows how there must always been something to occupy and something to be against. The oppression and genocide of Palestinians maintain those comforts under the occupation. If the Palestinians were free and equal under a democracy, a true democracy, one that wasn't based in ethnosupremacy and racism and inequity, then the benefits of the IOF would become forfeit. And like America's empire, to maintain the war machine is to maintain those who contribute to the war machine. There must always be those desperate enough to kill another. Give them reason to be desperate. Take their food for a day and let them starve. Toss their medication and tell them their money isn't enough. Destroy their education so that all they're taught is to hate the other. And once of all these factors become their reality, then they're led to the pseudo-career that the empire has made entirely coherent to their lives: the military. Join the military, and all these problems will become nonexistent. As long as you become our sword and kill without question. And you may argue there is no draft in America unlike apartheid Israel. But that's what makes America so horrifingly beautiful in its domination: the choice is an illusion here. Because deep down the rich know that even the poorest of us will become so desperate that we'll join the machine and kill for the comforts that come with the military. No matter the cost, as long as our killings are so far from us and incapable of hearing the screams of black and brown and asian children, men, and women will we be satisfied with our doings.

Water

Monday, September 7, 2026

I won't make this long. I went hiking near the Appalachian Mountains with my roommate today. We foraged for mushrooms, found Lion's Mane, the one mushroom that's named after chicken, and she gifted me a woodear mushroom. As we were walking back to the car, I asked if there was a creek I could sit at just to listen to the water move. The wind was so silent that every slice of water moving through the rocks was music. Growing up near the beach in Florida was a wild privilege. I spent much of my perceived childhood there with my best friends. My roommate told me the forest reminded her of those happiest of memories, many filled with the wild love she had for her best friend. I told her the water reminded me of mine. Every beach memory of him reflected in the moving water of that creek. I miss him. I miss him, and I hope one day I can move on and simply love the memory of my best friend, the one whose memories live in the water beneath me.

So, why don't I just de-transition? Well, I actually want to keep living. And I know if I de-transitioned, I'd just inevitably kill myself. And while transitioning has been an ultimate saving grace, America's constant hatred and vitriol against anyone transgender makes being alive so much worse. Any space that requires me to reveal my gender identity is met with so much hatred. This is most apparent in potential workplaces that are able to undergo background checks. If they look past my Muslim name and brown skin, I still have the worry of a final test of discrimination that is my gender. I can be the most qualified candidate with the perfect amount of experience, but I'm also what I like to call "too diverse" within spaces that I dream of working in.

A white or white-passing cis man with a high school degree can be considered somewhat of an equal candidate to a brown-skinned, Muslim transgender woman with a Master's degree, years of relevant experience, and a genuine love for the work she does. I say this mostly because this is exactly what happened to the job I had before being laid off. I had finished the same program my supervisor (the one with the high school degree as well as being two years younger than me) had completed the year prior that allowed us to spend a year at an organization with the possibility of being hired afterwards. This is a local non-profit in Baltimore that pivoted towards a dependence on federal, state, and private grants rather than prioritize small-based local donations. My supervisor's work program year was met with, in majority, a lot of assistance from our mutual boss. Our boss, or his at the time, technically, worked with him to establish partnerships with private entities that ultimately settled a role for him to continue to work there. In other words, the responsibility of the team was met with success because they assisted his infancy year with direct contribution. Now, this non-profit is also extremely small. At the time, my supervisor's supervisor was another peer with a director role and massive amounts of expected responsbility, but I'll just say now that his supervisor did not assist him at all and was shortly put on work probation for being a lacking employee. His supervisor had also been working there for several years. So, our boss becomes his symbolic supervisor while the actual supervisor maintains his own statute of failures. Our boss assists my supervisor with legal documentation, involves the board to provide increased assistance, and eventually lands a partnership with a private entity that provides grants to MY supervisor to maintain his career status at this non-profit.

Cut to my year. As further context, MY supervisor's role was designed by our boss. MY role was designed by my supervisor's supervisor...the one who was on work probation and designated a hinderance. Yeah. What I'm trying to say is, my role's description had very little to work with. I didn't know which direction I was meant to go with, and every time I asked my supervisor's supervisor (and this is getting confusing so let's just call my supervisor's supervisor Caleb, as Caleb was my initial supervisor before getting fired and eventually my new supervisor, who is the white-passing cis man with a high school degree, that we'll call Jacob, replaced Caleb). Sorry for the confusion. I don't really have the money to get sued for defamation now that I'm unemployed and trying to scrounge for pennies.

Caleb was Jacob's supervisor his entire year, did practically nothing for him, everyone knew, and still maintained his supervisory role for my year. "Well, what were ya wearin'?"

Saturday, June 20, 2026

I'm staring at the sky on this small hill in Patterson Park. It smells of nothing. Cleanliness. Air. Absent of coal and tar filling my lungs. My lungs still refrain from a full breath given the years of damage they've taken. My asthma still remains, my respiration a fraction of the childhood full of forced inhalation of nicotine smoke and burning tea bags.

I digress. There is never such a thing as a deserved peace, it seems. Creator works in ways beyond my imagining and tests us when we believe we are safe, even in the quiet of daylight grass. On a bench of many. Aside a cement walkway shaded by the climbing tree I jumped from just minutes before. Refrain. Quiet. Cleanliness. All at a cost.

Men are aberrations from the construct of chaos. They sow machinations upon us that destroy, the patriarchal divinity of malice and undoing. I am sitting on this bench watching the sky meld with its clouds. I am sitting beneath the leafshade of the tree canopy, somber and absent, yet present and aware. All good things behave in balance. Bad, or perceived evil, that is, must also makes it appearance.

A man approaches in my peripheral. He looks like the street sleepers who deserve housing and mental health access. And food. And education. His skin is melanin-rich, a shade of soil like mine, before dirt is watered yet dewy, full of discoloration beneath clothing. I believe it's called a "farmer's tan". But he is like me. Latin, Spanish-speaking, yet a man. A man who perceives certain aspects of people as fruit to eat for his own accord. Misdirected, misrepresented, misallocated rage. He wears a sweaty navy t-shirt and long pants. He hunches as he walks, his backpack slipping into his thin frame and outward from his stressed back. Even as I stare into the sky, I can feel the familiar grin of a hungering wraith. A blackguard approaches, I hear the creator whisper into my ear.

"Hello," he says in that accented English, like my mother's brothers in Ecuador. Pervasive, though. Expecting.

I stare in the sky, nodding. I do not respond. I am somber right now. I want to be alone. In my own space. Away from everybody.

"Hello," he says again, and his neck is bending in a stare, his elbows against his knees, his legs spread eagle.

"I know you can hear me."

I nod, my body's subconscious mannerisms reacting as they've been propagandized to. Mannerly. Civilized. Womanly........

But I am not afraid. My somber is transmutating. It is appearing at the edifice of a hilltop, nay, mountaintop, where blood fuses into facial apparations and boils into that ever-equalizing rage. Why must he bother me? Why must he expect reverence? I stare into the sky, moreso, and the clouds are disappearing. I do not hear anyone around us, the teenagers sitting meters from us on their own bench, the butch black lady with headphones on is walking farther away, and I realize I was more absent than I wanted to be. I am alone right now. Alone with a god apparent.

"What's your name?" he asks, and he's thirsting, this man, this abomination of the creator, that demanding undertone, the offspring of domination. Blackguard. Bastard.

I start to undo. There are keys between my fingers that were there to unlock my bike beside me. A fickle finger play begins, and I twist them between the gloves I wear. Metal against metal, the ring of artifical urgency, yet I sit. This is but another game that I must win.

"I know you can hear me. What's your name?" he says, fickle, annoyed, still sitting on his own bench beside my own.

My skin is warm, my visage turning. I chuckle by the nonsense that is occuring, but it melds into a waiting smear. I am ready. Whatever is to happen, will happen by the supposed consequence of the creator. I am merely a facsimile of resistance, a stardust comet of rebellion, the spawn of my mother and our mothers before her. Fear is a tool of control, yes. But it is also a weapon to react. I am reacting. I am waiting. I am the godkiller.

There are keys between my fingers, and I tighten my grip. My subconscious knows to smile, but it also knows to scream. It knows to bleed. It knows how to kill.

"I am George. What is your name?" he asks again. He waits. I shake my head slowly, and the smile returns, my smile, yet it is twisted, a mimicry of his own, reactive, countering, a mirror of insanity.

He stands. "Can I sit next you?" he asks, pointing to the empty space beside me. Closer he comes. Sterile. Scentless. Empty.

There is a moment when every transgender woman must come to her own conclusion of reality that we live in a country that wants us to die in whichever way we can. It is a convenience to the government and the authority as our very existence is resistance and our freedoms are an evolution for all others. Suicide, murder, disappearances. These are all preferred conclusions to our ends. Yet there is a moment when a transgender woman must also realize that no one will come to save her, no matter the circumstance, no matter the creator's whims. We are the godkillers in our reality. We must slay the slayer. And here, I tell her, a god approaches.

He walks forward, closer, and I turn my head from the sky for the very first time and stare at him through my black sunglasses and say, "No, por favor. Sale de aqui, por favor." How mannerly, everpresent.

She is human, he thinks. An echo of revelation occurs to him. Dark skin is seen as a fetish to the white man. And the white man teaches our fathers what is considered appropriate, no matter how damning. We are seen as a consumption of sorts, with how our bodies have been related to delicacies for the tasting. But when a Spanish man who speaks like my uncles and talks like my cousins assumes a doll is simply a doll to play with with no ability or capacity of self-thought, he is, in and of itself, vulnerable.

She speaks. Yes, I do. And I speak your language. My language. Our language. A connection is made with this god and godkiller, and he leaves. He nods and is in fear. It is likely he thought I was a sex worker waiting for a client, or, maybe he wanted to steal my bike with threats. I didn't include that bit of dialogue for the narrative, but he did complement my bike several times. No, no, no. Truthfully, though, I wanted him to attack. I wanted to retaliate to the highest of my power, no matter how relatively small my physical prowess was to his. I wanted to beat the man. I wanted to watch him bleed or die trying. I wanted to release my anger on such a foul copy of a man. Bitter thing that walked away.

I pity the fool for missing the discovery of my fire. I would've burned every bit of his flesh just to enact whatever justice I saw fit. And there I was again. Missing the movement of the sky. Wind bleeding through branches and tree leaves. The great giant covering me in its shade. The creator reminded me that my voice has power. My silence was my weakness. My shout, a weapon to fell a god.

Yet, still, I remain unsatisfied.

I Got Hit By A Car

Friday, May 8, 2026

A lot of American exceptionalism and classist theology teaches us to side with the oppressor and always question the victim or the oppressed. With that being said, no, I was not at fault. I was riding my bike in New York City, around the lower part of Manhattan with my partner and friend, and we had not only the right of way, but were as visible as we could be with the amount of lights we had. The driver not only was blocking both the crosswalk and side street, but he also broke several traffic laws by being a hazard for folks not in cars. He initially backed his car up, and I interpreted that as him not only seeing us but giving us space to go in front of him, and our friend Ibrahim did actually ride in front of him. Alas, when it was my turn to ride in front of his car, he immediately accelerated into me, and I was launched off my bike, into the New York City streets where my forehead and asphalt made contact with each other, causing me to be severely concussed, and the driver eventually drove away. Ergo, a hit and run. He initially got out of his car to make sure I wasn't dead and that he didn't commit murder, and when he noticed I was breathing, and my partner told him to pull aside so we could get his information, he instead drove off, because, well, god forbid you hold yourself accountable for nearly killing someone.

If you ask me, he committed attempted murder. There were no cameras at this intersection, it was busy as fuck, car drivers in every corner caught in evening congestion, blasting their horns, and even the guy whose car who I was launched into, my frame hitting his rear light, made time to get out not to check on me but to CHECK HIS CAR, also dipped because, well, god forbid we care more about people than property.

No, we didn't take any pictures of the assailant. We had some hope for humanity that he'd pull over and provide his information. No, the police have yet to find him. No, I don't think he'll ever be found. My glasses were broken on the fall, and I have a concussion. My entire body is in pain, I have bruises everywhere, and I'm honestly fortunate enough to not be dead or have broken bones. Even if I did die, American society has developed such a violent malice towards the impoverished and those who are unable to drive or afford to drive that the likely response from car drivers would be, "Well, it's your fault for being on the road on a bike". Never does the status quo question itself. Never does society reflect onto itself and the standards that we have been given. I am genuinely grateful I am not dead. If I was, that'd make the authority's response to my death far easier because I am ultimately silenced and can be spoken for not by myself but another who can easily twist the entire situation against my favor.

The bike is fine. My front wheel is destroyed, but it's fine. It was a new wheel---pink Velocity rim---and so were my glasses, of which I had just received just two weeks prior to the crash (I initially typed accident because we are propagandized to call these crashes 'accidents', and that language therefore takes away ANY accountability from the assailant/murderer). My mind is all over the place. It's hard for me to keep a single thought. I've been increasingly more forgetful. It's hard to maintain conversation. I honestly am afraid I've developed a traumatic brain injury of some sort. I hate being vulnerable and weakened outside of my own accord. But I'm alive. Gods, I'm alive. And it's angering. It's angering that a hit-and-run can just occur with little impunity, and I am forced to continue my life as if nothing had happened. America is such a reactive society. If I died, then there'd be some sort of response that night to hold the man accountable. But since I survived, nothing occurred. "Oh, she's breathing". I'm still injured, though. I'm still bleeding. I'm still in pain. I'm still broken, mentally, emotionally. How many more times does the system think we'll allow injustice to pursue? There is a breaking point amongst these things where even the individual reaches their limit and demands accountability. When all is lost, there is nothing to lose.

Even after all of this, I still loved New York City. It's such a beautiful place. I don't care how stimulating it is for others. I felt so invisible, in the best way. There's a human saturation amongst every street that I just blend in. Save the street. In the streets I am the most visible. I am seen yet feel the most empowered. I am god on the streets, on my bike. It's so liberating. I think the reason why car drivers hate us so much is because they hate the system that has put them there, but they don't realize it. They want something visible to hate, and it's impossible to hate someone inside of a car because you can't even see them. But you can see the cyclist. You see how happy they are, how visible their emotions become, how exciting it is to see them be excited. And envy develops, and so on, and so on.

I hope they find the man. I do. And, yes, I will be suing. The guy did a hit-and-run. The guy almost killed me. And, if I have the choice, I want to see him behind bars for a long time. We need to stop letting attempted homicides like this slide just because America has taught us to blame the victim amongst every circumstance. No longer.

Free Palestine. Liberation for the working class. Tax the rich. Fuck the oppressors.
